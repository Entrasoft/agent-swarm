# Agent cooperation research watch

Established September 15, 2026. Publication and further experiments are deferred.

## Purpose and cadence

A scheduled follow-up in the existing Codex task checks monthly, on the first
day of the month at 9:00 a.m. local time. It follows outside research that could
change the case for a substantive, affordable study of agent cooperation in
mathematical or scientific discovery. It is not a general AI-news digest or an
automatic experiment runner.

The watch uses the task's ordinary research resources. It does not use the
project's experimental API key or authorize new experiment spending. Local
scheduled work requires the computer and app to be running.

## Findings worth surfacing

- Repeated, independently verified performance benefits against strong solo or
  isolated-worker baselines with comparable total resources.
- Traceable examples of agents checking, correcting, or extending one another's
  intermediate work, supported by public artifacts and independent verification.
- Controlled evidence about agent count, interaction depth, tools, persistence,
  and cost that clarifies what might transfer to a modest team.
- Reproducible mechanisms with released prompts, code, and evaluators, clearly
  separating prescribed workflows from organization that was not assigned.
- Informative negative findings or independent replications that meaningfully
  narrow the promising approaches or strengthen the case to remain paused.

No single paper must satisfy every item. Each alert must explain which decision
it changes and identify evidence limitations. Agent count, message volume,
self-reported reuse, a fitted scaling curve, or a headline alone is insufficient.
Keep consensus, common-goal scientific collaboration, and mixed-motive social
dilemmas distinct. Do not transfer results from learning agents to fixed-model
inference without explaining the difference.

## Reporting and boundaries

Review at most five promising primary papers or evidence releases per check.
Focus on new work or material revisions since the previous check, and avoid
repeating sources already assessed. Remain quiet when nothing changes the
decision. A substantive alert should include at most three findings, with
primary links, the result, important limitations, reported resources where
available, and the implication for remaining paused or considering a bounded
follow-up. A blocking monitoring failure may also warrant an alert.

Do not change repository files, rewrite or publish drafts, contact researchers,
or run experiments. Preserve the original hypothesis and historical outcomes.
Any later experiment needs a specific question, a novel contribution relative
to prior work, a defensible design, and an explicit resource decision.
